FSE Opening Keynote's Prop


In 2026, I was invited to give the opening keynote at the International Conference on the Foundations of Software Engineering (FSE). A unique opportunity to prepare a talk for a broad, expert software engineering audience, and to design a tailor-made prop for the talk.

The talk, titled "Punkin'up Dependency Hell", was about how dependency hell has grown into a wide and rich software supply chain composed of tools, libraries, pipelines and millions of lines third-party code, usually open source. The punk refered to the hardly-tamed benefits and wicked caveats that come with this supply chain. Punk also set the aesthetics for the prop.

The key idea was to reproduce a mundane dependency declaration file, seed for a key part of the software supply chain, with a punk visual aesthetic. First, we chose a real-world dependency file. We selected the Cargo file of the glicol live coding environment. We had two motivations for this choice: toml is a realtive compact format, and we love the unique blend of code, art and performance of live coding!

With a clear spec, reproduce glicol's cargo.toml with punk visual aesthetics, we just had to do it. For a few weeks, the research krew collected the hardware: paper-made magazines, analog newspapers, supermarket coupons and discounts leaflets, and other unusual sources of diverse textures, colors and fonts. A few weeks before FSE, we gathered with all this material, tape, glue, scisors, snacks to implement the spec. For one afternoon, we manipulated pure tangible material, looking for characters, one after the other, patiently, with no ctrl-F. Little by little, we assembled 12 lines of the dependency file, on an A3 black page.

As the final prop was going to be a t-shirt for all members of the krew, we also designed a back. It is a fake tour schedule for the imaginary dependency punk band. The tour goes in certain cities and countries, at certain dates. All place names are names of real software packages that are named after places, and the dates are the dates of the latest release of each package as of June 10, 2026. The schedule is available here
